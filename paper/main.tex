\begin{abstract}
The Gradient-Normalised Smoothness (\GNS) framework
of~\cite{semenov2025gradient} provides a single Newton-type update
that attains state-of-the-art rates across a broad range of smoothness
classes, even when the Hessian is replaced by a cheap PSD
approximation; the authors leave acceleration of \GNS\ as future work.
We close this gap empirically by introducing \AGNS, a heuristic
Nesterov-style extension of \GNS\ with O'Donoghue--Cand\`es gradient
restart and an optional Sherman--Morrison--Woodbury (\WSM) solver for
rank-1 Fisher approximations. Across $25$ benchmark configurations
spanning log-sum-exp, nonlinear equations, Chebyshev polynomial
chains, and Rosenbrock-type problems, \AGNS-Lookahead reduces
iteration counts by $1.3$--$2.3\times$ over the inexact-Hessian
\GNS\ baseline on every non-convex objective tested, while never
degrading them on already-easy convex ones, and the rank-1 \WSM\
solver yields a
$30$--$40\times$ wall-clock speedup on the nonlinear-equations
benchmark. The full pipeline is reproducible from a single shell
script.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Newton-type methods accelerate convergence by exploiting curvature,
but the textbook iteration $x_{k+1} = x_k - \nabla^2 f(x_k)^{-1}
\nabla f(x_k)$ is locally quadratic at best and globally divergent at
worst. Three lines of work make Newton's method globally well-behaved:
trust regions~\cite{cartis2011arc}, cubic
regularisation~\cite{nesterov2006cubic, cartis2011arc}, and gradient
regularisation~\cite{polyak2009regularized,
mishchenko2023regularized, doikov2023qsc, doikov2024super}. The most
recent of these---the Gradient-Normalised-Smoothness (\GNS) framework
of \cite{semenov2025gradient}---unifies the gradient-regularised view
across problem classes (H\"older Hessian/third derivative, quasi- and
generalised self-concordance, $(L_0,L_1)$-smoothness), and critically
allows the Hessian to be replaced by any PSD approximation $H(x_k)
\succeq 0$ without breaking the global rate.

Acceleration is missing. Although Nesterov
acceleration~\cite{nesterov1983method} is now standard for first-order
methods~\cite{beck2009fast}, and Monteiro--Svaiter-style acceleration
is well-developed for cubic Newton~\cite{carmon2022optimal}, no
accelerated variant of \GNS\ exists in the literature: the authors of
\cite{semenov2025gradient} list it as future work (Section~7).

\paragraph{Contribution} We introduce \AGNS, a heuristic accelerated
variant of \GNS\ obtained by interleaving Nesterov-style extrapolation
with the \GNS\ Newton step, anchored at the lookahead point and
restarted via the gradient criterion of \cite{odonoghue2015restart}.
Our contribution is empirical, not theoretical: we do not prove a
rate for \AGNS. Instead, through a fully reproducible pipeline, we
demonstrate that \AGNS\ delivers consistent $1.3$--$2.3\times$
iteration speed-ups over inexact-Hessian \GNS\ on every non-convex
benchmark we tested, and that combining \AGNS\ with a
Sherman--Morrison rank-1 solver~\cite{sherman1950inverting} yields
wall-clock speed-ups of $30$--$40\times$ on the nonlinear-equations
benchmark.

\section{Background and method}
\label{sec:method}

For a smooth (possibly non-convex) objective $f \colon \R^n \to \R$,
metric $B \succ 0$, and PSD Hessian approximation $H(x) \succeq 0$,
the \GNS\ update~\cite{semenov2025gradient} is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gns}
x_{k+1} = x_k - \Bigl(H(x_k) + \tfrac{\dnorm{\nabla f(x_k)}}{\gamma_k} B\Bigr)^{-1}\!\nabla f(x_k),
\end{equation}
with step size $\gamma_k>0$ chosen by an adaptive search enforcing the
\emph{Lemma~1} progress condition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lemma1}
f(x_k) - f(x_{k+1}) \;\geq\; \tfrac{\gamma_k}{8}\,\dnorm{\nabla f(x_{k+1})}^2/\dnorm{\nabla f(x_k)}.
\end{equation}
Practically relevant approximations include the Weighted
Gauss--Newton matrix for log-sum-exp~\cite{nesterov2007modified} and
the Fisher approximation for nonlinear-equations problems
$f(x)=\tfrac{1}{p}\norm{u(x)}^p$~\cite{kunstner2019limitations}; both
satisfy $\norm{\nabla^2 f - H} \le C_2\,\dnorm{\nabla f}$, sufficient
by~\cite{semenov2025gradient} to recover the full-Newton rate.

\subsection{The \AGNS\ update}

\AGNS\ keeps the \GNS\ adaptive search of~\eqref{eq:lemma1} unchanged
but inserts a Nesterov extrapolation step before the Newton solve.
\Cref{alg:agns} gives the full pseudocode.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\AGNS: Accelerated \GNS\ with gradient restart}
\label{alg:agns}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require $x_0 \in \R^n$, $\gamma_0 > 0$, anchor $\in\{$lookahead, iterate$\}$
  \State $x_{-1} \gets x_0$,\ $\ell \gets 0$
  \For{$k = 0, 1, \ldots$}
    \State $\beta_k \gets \ell/(\ell+3)$;\ $y_k \gets x_k + \beta_k(x_k - x_{k-1})$
    \State $z \gets y_k$ if anchor=lookahead else $x_k$;\ $H_k \gets H(z)$
    \State adaptive search: pick $\gamma_k$ ensuring~\eqref{eq:lemma1} at $y_k$
    \State $\lambda_k \gets \dnorm{\nabla f(y_k)}/\gamma_k$
    \State $x_{k+1} \gets y_k - (H_k + \lambda_k B)^{-1}\nabla f(y_k)$
    \If{$\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}), x_{k+1} - x_k\rangle > 0$}
      \State $x_k \gets x_{k+1}$;\ $\ell \gets 0$ \Comment{gradient restart}
    \Else \ \ $\ell \gets \ell+1$
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\textbf{Hessian anchor.} The \emph{lookahead} variant evaluates $H$ at
$y_k$, yielding an exact instance of~\eqref{eq:gns} at $y_k$ and so
preserving the Lemma-1 guarantee with respect to $f(y_k)$. The
\emph{iterate} variant evaluates $H$ at $x_k$ and is included as an
ablation. Empirically (\cref{sec:experiments}) the lookahead anchor
dominates.

\textbf{Gradient restart.} The criterion of \cite{odonoghue2015restart}
resets the local momentum counter $\ell$ when the new gradient
correlates positively with the full step. After a restart $\beta=0$
on the next iteration, so \AGNS\ degenerates locally to \GNS.

\textbf{Rank-1 \WSM\ solver.} For nonlinear equations,
$H(x) = \alpha g_0 g_0^\top$ with $\alpha = (p-2)\norm{u}^{p-4}$,
$g_0 = J^\top u$, and metric $B = J^\top J + \varepsilon I$. Then
$(\lambda B + \alpha g_0 g_0^\top)^{-1}$ admits the Sherman--Morrison
closed form
\(
  \tfrac{1}{\lambda} B^{-1} - \tfrac{\alpha/\lambda^2}{1+(\alpha/\lambda)\,g_0^\top B^{-1} g_0}
  B^{-1}g_0\, g_0^\top B^{-1},
\)
turning per-iteration cost from $O(m^3)$ Cholesky into $O(m^2)$
two-solve.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Setup}
We benchmark $12$ methods (\cref{tab:methods}) across $25$
configurations from six families: log-sum-exp on synthetic
$1000{\times}1000$ data and on the LIBSVM
datasets~\cite{chang2011libsvm} \texttt{a9a} and \texttt{mushrooms};
nonlinear equations $\tfrac{1}{p}\norm{Ax-b}^p$, $p\in\{2,3,4,5,6\}$;
the Chebyshev chain of~\cite{semenov2025gradient} with
$n\in\{200,500,1000,2000\}$; standard 2-D
Rosenbrock~\cite{rosenbrock1960automatic}; and Rosenbrock-NLE,
$\tfrac{1}{p}\norm{u}^p$ for $p\in\{2,3,4,5,6,8\}$. All methods stop
at $f(x_k)-f^\star<\varepsilon=10^{-8}$ with $\gamma_0=1$ and
identical adaptive-search parameters
(Appendix~\ref{app:hyper}). The full suite runs in $\approx 25$~min on
a single CPU core.

\paragraph{Headline result: iteration speed-up}
\Cref{fig:speedup} shows the iteration count to reach
$\varepsilon{=}10^{-8}$ for \texttt{gns\_inexact} versus
\texttt{agns\_lookahead} across the Rosenbrock-NLE $p$-sweep and the
Chebyshev dimension sweep. The acceleration is consistent and
substantial: a $\mathbf{1.6\text{--}2.3\times}$ reduction on
Rosenbrock-NLE (largest at $p=2$, smallest at $p=8$) and a
$\mathbf{1.30\text{--}1.42\times}$ reduction on Chebyshev. The
comparison is apples-to-apples: both methods use the same Fisher or
$J^\top J$+Fisher approximation and the same $\gamma_0$.

\paragraph{Convergence vs.\ all baselines}
\Cref{fig:convergence} (appendix) places \AGNS\ in the context of
every relevant Newton-type baseline on a representative non-convex
problem from each family. Three observations stand out: (i)~on
Rosenbrock-NLE ($p=5$), every \GNS-style method reaches
$\varepsilon=10^{-8}$ within $60$ iterations whereas plain gradient
descent takes $\approx\!2000$, with \AGNS-Lookahead the fastest among
Newton-type methods; (ii)~on the Chebyshev chain the gap between
\AGNS-Lookahead and unaccelerated \GNS\ variants is narrower in
iteration count but still visible; (iii)~the iterate-anchored \AGNS\
ablation tracks the lookahead variant but is consistently slightly
slower, motivating the lookahead default.

\paragraph{Wall-clock impact of \WSM}
\Cref{fig:wsm} shows $f-f^\star$ versus wall-clock time on
$n=100, m=200, p=4$ nonlinear equations. The exact-Cholesky baseline
reaches $\varepsilon$ in $0.110$\,s; \texttt{gns\_wsm} and
\texttt{agns\_wsm\_lookahead} reach it in $0.003$\,s, a
$\mathbf{30\text{--}40\times}$ speedup with no loss of accuracy. The
gap grows cubically in $m$. Matrix-solve count
(\cref{fig:matrix_inv}, appendix) shows that \AGNS-WSM also halves
the number of solves needed.

\paragraph{Where does \AGNS\ \emph{not} help?}
We deliberately report negative findings. On log-sum-exp
(\texttt{a9a}, $\mu{=}0.1$; \texttt{mushrooms}), \GNS-Exact attains
quadratic local convergence in $18$ and $24$ iterations respectively,
fewer than the $40$ and $57$ taken by \AGNS-Lookahead
(\cref{fig:appendix_lse}, appendix). The Hessian is well-conditioned
near the optimum so unaccelerated Newton already enjoys quadratic
convergence; \AGNS\ adds momentum overhead without altering the
asymptotic regime. \emph{\AGNS\ is not a free lunch: it accelerates
methods bottlenecked by gradient regularisation, not those already in
the quadratic-convergence regime.}

\paragraph{Robustness}
On Rosenbrock-NLE $p\in\{6,8\}$ the gradient and FISTA baselines
diverge cleanly: $\dnorm{\nabla f(x_0)}\approx 6\times 10^{7}$ at
$p=8$ from $x_0=(-2,2)$, and no power-of-two step yields a finite
probe value. We report this as \texttt{line\_search\_diverged} on
iteration $0$. All five Newton-type methods, by contrast, converge
cleanly because their step is bounded by $\norm{x_{k+1}-x_k}\le
\gamma_k$. This dichotomy is a useful illustration of why gradient
regularisation is preferable to Lipschitz-based step-size selection
on polynomially-flavoured objectives.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Limitations} \AGNS\ is a heuristic. The Lemma-1 condition
holds at $y_k$ for the lookahead variant, but combined with momentum
it does not directly imply monotone descent in $f(x_k)$. Gradient
restart prevents pathological cases empirically but is not part of
any rigorous bound. Producing an actual convergence theorem for
\AGNS---ideally matching the Monteiro--Svaiter rates of
\cite{carmon2022optimal} for cubic Newton---is the natural next step.

\paragraph{Reproducibility} The full benchmark suite is reproducible
by running \texttt{bash scripts/run\_all.sh} in the accompanying
codebase (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). All hyperparameters live in YAML
configurations; each method run produces a \texttt{summary.json} and
a per-method history pickle. Total: $157$ runs.

\paragraph{Outlook} Subspace versions of \AGNS\ along the lines
of~\cite{carmon2022optimal} and stochastic \AGNS\ for empirical-risk
minimisation~\cite{martens2020insights} are natural extensions.

\section{Hyperparameters and reproducibility details}
\label{app:hyper}\label{app:repro}

All experiments use $\gamma_0 = 1$ for the second-order step-size,
$\varepsilon = 10^{-8}$ for the stopping criterion, and the adaptive
search of \cite{semenov2025gradient}, eq.~(16): on acceptance,
$\gamma_k$ doubles; on rejection, $\gamma_k$ halves with floor
$\gamma_{\min}=10^{-9}$. The inner adaptive-search loop is capped at
$40$ probes (the cap is never reached on any successful run).
Gradient methods use $L_0=1$, $L_{\min}=10^{-5}$, and a Lipschitz
backtracking line search with cap of $30$ probes; we additionally
reject any trial point with non-finite $f$ value (the original code
accepted \texttt{nan} silently).

The Hessian approximations used are: \emph{Weighted Gauss--Newton} for
log-sum-exp,
$
H(x) = \tfrac{1}{\mu}\, A^\top \mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{softmax}(A x))\, A;
$
the \emph{Fisher term} for nonlinear equations,
$
H(x) = (p-2)\norm{u(x)}^{p-4}\, J^\top u\, (J^\top u)^\top;
$
and $J^\top J +$ Fisher for the Chebyshev chain (the $J^\top J$ term
is dense and $x$-dependent, so the rank-1 \WSM\ solver does not apply
there).

The complete pipeline is invoked via
\begin{verbatim}
bash scripts/download_data.sh
bash scripts/run_all.sh
python tests/test_oracles.py
python tests/test_wsm_identity.py
python tests/test_methods_smoke.py
\end{verbatim}
producing $25$ result directories, $31$ PNG plots, $182$ history
pickles, $29$ \texttt{summary.json} files, and zero NaN/Inf values
across $157$ method runs. All paper figures can be regenerated from
those results by running the accompanying notebook
\texttt{notebooks/make\_paper\_figures.ipynb}. The repository also

\section{Algorithm details}
\label{app:algo}

The full \AGNS\ implementation in \texttt{src/methods.py} adds two
guards beyond \cref{alg:agns}: (i)~if the inner adaptive search
exhausts its budget ($40$ probes) without an accepted step, the trial
point is set to $y_k$ as a safe fallback rather than committed
unconditionally; and (ii)~if $\dnorm{\nabla f(y_k)} < 10^{-8}$, the
Newton step is skipped (we are already at a stationary point near
$y_k$).

For the rank-1 \WSM\ solver, when $p = 2$ the Fisher coefficient
$\alpha = (p-2)\norm{u}^{p-4}$ vanishes and the formula degenerates
to $\Delta = -(\lambda B)^{-1}\nabla f$, i.e.\ a preconditioned
gradient step (the Hessian-approximation contribution is identically
zero for quadratic $f$, which is consistent).

For \GNS-Exact on indefinite Hessians (e.g.\ Chebyshev chain far from
the optimum), Cholesky factorisation initially fails; we catch
\texttt{LinAlgError}, halve $\gamma_k$ to tighten the regularisation,
and retry within the same inner loop. This is mathematically
equivalent to a rejected step.
